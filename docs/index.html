Android · BLE · Modbus TCP · Pose estimation

Jog a robot arm from your phone. Or just wave at it.

Jog The Robot is a Kotlin / Jetpack Compose app that moves a 5-axis igus robolink-style arm. Hold a button to jog an axis, or stand in front of the camera and let an on-device YOLO pose model turn your arm positions into jog commands.

What this project is

A complete phone-to-robot control chain: the Android app, the ESP32 firmware that bridges Bluetooth to the PLC, and the PLC logic that drives the arm.

Jog

Pick an axis (A1 to A5), then hold Left or Right. The command repeats every 100 ms while you hold and a stop is sent the moment you let go.

Gesture Jog

The front camera feeds YOLO26n-pose (ONNX Runtime, CPU or NNAPI). Seventeen body keypoints become axis selection and jog direction, no touch needed.

Safe by default

A 300 ms deadman on the ESP32, a heartbeat watchdog to the PLC, and jog buttons that stay disabled unless the PLC is online and ready.

How it works

Every jog travels through four hops. Status flows back the other way, so the app always shows what the PLC is actually doing.

1

Connect

The app scans for 15 s and flags devices advertising the Nordic UART service. Pick JogTheRobot; it is saved for next time. On connect the app requests an MTU of 185 and subscribes to notifications.

2

Power on and home

The status card shows the PLC state (Power off, Powered not homed, Ready…). Flip the Power switch (P1), then tap Home (H). Jogging unlocks once the robot is Ready.

3

Jog

Tap a joint on the arm drawing or an axis button, then hold Left or Right. The app sends A2:R every 100 ms, the ESP32 puts axis and direction into Modbus input registers, and the PLC moves the arm.

4

Release to stop

Letting go sends S. If the phone goes quiet for 300 ms or disconnects, the ESP32 stops the jog on its own.

Gesture Jog sequence

Each camera frame is letterboxed to 640×640 and run through the pose model. Gestures follow a fixed order: neutral, then an axis pose, then a turn pose to jog.

PoseCommandEffect
Both arms hanging straight downNEUTRALArms the selector and clears the axis
Arms crossed above the head (X)STOPSelect A1
T-pose, both arms horizontalSTARTSelect A2
Both hands up in a V above the headFORWARDSelect A3
Both arms down at ~45°, hands apartBACKWARDSelect A4
Left arm horizontal, right arm downTURN_LEFTJog selected axis Left
Right arm horizontal, left arm downTURN_RIGHTJog selected axis Right
No person in frameNO_PERSONClear the axis and stop

While a turn pose is held the jog repeats every 100 ms. If no frame shows it for 400 ms, the loop ends and sends S, so inference needs to stay above about 3 FPS. On a Nothing Phone (A015) with NNAPI FP32 it runs at about 6 FPS.

Under the hood

Plain ASCII lines over BLE, ending in \n. Both sides buffer until a newline because BLE packets can split or join lines.
